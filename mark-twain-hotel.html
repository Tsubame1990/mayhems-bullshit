The Mark Twain Hotel

The Mark Twain looks great from a distance. Nine stories of pale brick gone gray at the seams, the name spelled out in a vertical sign that probably glowed proudly sometime around the Eisenhower administration and now buzzes intermittently.

The lobby does its best to impress, and for a moment it succeeds. The front desk is manned by a single tired clerk who looks up, nods, and goes back to whatever holds their attention on the screen — no hard questions here.

The elevator arrives with a groan and a shudder, its indicator light stuttering between floors, and the hallway it opens onto smells faintly of old carpet and older cigarette smoke baked into the walls.
